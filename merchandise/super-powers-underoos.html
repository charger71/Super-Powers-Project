Super Powers Underoos

Super Powers merchandise · Fruit of the Loom · 1984

Photography

About

"Underwear that's fun to wear" — character-emblazoned Underoos sets under the Super Powers banner, among the most remembered soft-goods tie-ins of the era.

The 1984 cross-promotion between Fruit of the Loom’s Underoos and Kenner’s Super Powers Collection stands as one of the most cohesive marketing strategies of the 1980s. By synchronizing product lines, the two companies shifted character underwear from generic novelty items into a direct extension of a child's toy chest.

The Kenner Connection and Packaging Layout

When Kenner launched the Super Powers Collection in 1984, they dominated toy aisles with action figures featuring mechanical "Power Action" features. To capitalize on this frenzy, Fruit of the Loom repackaged their traditional DC Comics Underoos to explicitly match the toy line's aesthetic.The packaging was altered to mirror Kenner's distinct card backs:

The Comic Border: Packages swapped out standard branding for the bright yellow-and-red starburst border used on the action figure pegs.

The Garcia-López Artwork: The undershirts used the definitive DC Comics style guide artwork illustrated by José Luis García-López, which was identical to the art printed on the front of the Kenner action figure cards.

Character Lineups: The primary initial wave directly mirrored the anchor heroes of Kenner's Series 1 and Series 2 toy releases. This included Superman, Batman, Robin, and Wonder Woman.

Design Choices: Costume Mimicry vs. Screen Prints

Fruit of the Loom utilized two distinct design methodologies for the DC Super Powers line, depending on the complexity of the hero's look.

The "Fun Pals" Competitive Shift

During this era, Fruit of the Loom operated a multi-tiered children's underwear strategy. 

While Underoos were sold as high-quality, two-piece "costume" sets (shirt and bottoms), the company simultaneously ran the Fun Pals line. Fun Pals were sold in traditional multi-packs and featured smaller, basic character screen-prints directly onto standard white briefs. The premium Super Powers Underoos sets were intentionally positioned as a premium product, utilizing fully dyed fabrics and wrap-around torso prints to maximize the illusion of wearing a real superhero suit.

Modern Availability

While the original 1980s sets are now highly sought-after collectibles among vintage toy enthusiasts, the concept survives today. The Underoos brand was later licensed to pop-culture manufacturer Bioworld, which continues to manufacture classic retro DC Comics sets. These modern iterations preserve the original 1980s color schemes and García-López-era logos, though they are now widely produced in adult sizes for nostalgic fans.

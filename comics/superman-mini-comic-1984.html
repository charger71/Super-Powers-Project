About

Review Article: Kenner Super Powers Collection #1 — Superman

Published in 1984 as a promotional insert for Kenner’s iconic action figure line, the Super Powers Collection #1 minicomic served as a brief, self-contained showcase for Superman. Designed to engage young consumers and provide immediate narrative context for the accompanying toy, this 16-page comic delivers a straightforward, classic superhero conflict without the narrative baggage of mainstream monthly continuity. The book relies heavily on established character dynamics, providing a nostalgic look back at DC's Bronze Age era. 

Because these promotional inserts did not include official printed creator credits, historians attribute the creative work to prominent DC mainstays of the era. The interior pencils are largely attributed to Adrian Gonzales, while legendary artist Joe Giella handled the cover art and interior inks. The script itself was managed in-house by DC editors, heavily adapting toyline concepts originally established by Jack Kirby and Joey Cavalieri.

The plot follows a standard formula as Lex Luthor launches a surprise raid on the Metropolis Nuclear Power Station. Luthor's objective is to siphon the facility's immense nuclear energy to fully charge his high-tech, green-and-purple mechanical battle suit. Once powered up, the villain attempts to use his enhanced armor to permanently destroy Superman. However, the Man of Steel quickly intervenes, utilizing his traditional powers to counter Luthor’s offensive, neutralize the threat, and protect Metropolis from potential nuclear disaster.

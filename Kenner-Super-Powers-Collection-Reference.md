# The Kenner Super Powers Collection (1984–1986)
### A Collector's Reference & Media Guide

---

## Overview

The **Super Powers Collection** was Kenner's line of DC Comics action figures produced across three series from **1984 to 1986**. DC awarded Kenner the master toy license in 1982, taking it away from Mego (which had held the DC/Marvel license through the 1970s) and beating out Mattel — hot on the heels of Mattel's action-feature-driven *Masters of the Universe*. Kenner, fresh off its *Star Wars* juggernaut (which wound down the same year Super Powers ended), leaned into two strengths: **mechanical "action features"** and **high-fidelity comic art**.

The line's defining gimmick was a hidden spring mechanism: squeezing a figure's legs or arms triggered a signature motion — the figure's "super power." That mechanic is literally where the line got its name.

- **Scale:** ~4.5 inches (all-plastic bodies, decent articulation for the era). *Note: this is often misreported as 3¾".*
- **Run:** Three series — 1984, 1985, 1986. Cancelled abruptly in **February 1986**.
- **Final tally:** **34 figures** (arguably 35 with the Argentine Riddler), **8 vehicles**, **1 playset**, **1 carrying case**.
- **Why it ended:** The line was front-loaded with A-list characters, later waves leaned on lesser-known heroes, and the toy aisle got crowded (G.I. Joe, Transformers, M.A.S.K., Mattel's *Secret Wars*). Kenner also over-packed cases with core characters (reportedly up to 18 of one character per case of 24), creating a glut.

---

## What Made Them Distinctive

- **Power Action features** — each figure had one signature motion (e.g., Superman's Power Action Punch, Aquaman's Deep Sea Kick, Hawkman's flapping wings).
- **Definitive DC art** — most figure and packaging art was based on **José Luis García-López's** classic DC Style Guides, with additional artwork attributed to **Dick Giordano**.
- **Creator involvement (and rare royalties):**
  - **Jack Kirby** redesigned his "Fourth World" characters (Darkseid, Kalibak, Steppenwolf, DeSaad, Mantis, Parademon, Orion, Mister Miracle) for the toys — reportedly among the only royalties he ever received for his creations.
  - **George Pérez** designed Cyborg and redesigned Lex Luthor and Brainiac, and received royalties as well.
- **Mini-comics** — figures in the first two series shipped with a mini-comic spotlighting that character.

## Never Produced (Prototypes & Cancelled Items)

Pictured on the backs of third-wave cards but **never released**:

- **Darkseid's Tower of Darkness** — a Darkseid playset previewed at the 1986 Toy Fair. Design featured rotating cannons, clutching arms, and a revolving gate/jail cell. Reached late development.
- **All-Terrain Trapper Vehicle** — cancelled before production.

In 2003, toy historian **Jason Geyer** (ToyOtter) and collector James "Sallah" Sawyer revealed unmade designs for **Series Four, Five, and Six**, plus additional vehicles, playsets, and a deluxe **"Power Plus"** figure line.

- **Man-Bat** — the most famous unproduced figure; an actual hardcopy prototype was created.

## International Variants (a whole rabbit hole)

- **Brazil — Gulliver / Estrela:** produced their own Super Powers figures. Estrela also published Portuguese mini-comics for Cyborg, Plastic Man, and Shazam (characters that never got English mini-comics).
- **Argentina — Pacipa:** created a **Riddler** figure without Kenner's consent (a repainted Green Lantern mold). Only available in Argentina, it's the **rarest and most expensive** figure in the whole universe of Super Powers — routinely $2,000–$3,000.
- **Colombia — "Super Heroes Collection" brand:** **El Capitan Rayo / Captain Ray** (Aquaman head on a Superman body) and **Hombre de las Nieves / Abominable Snowman** (a white-and-gray Mego Pocket Heroes Hulk mold).
- Numerous other regional makers (Industrial Toy Werks, Punk & Pop Toys, Industria Argentina, etc.) released figures under "Super Powers," "Super Amigos," or "Super Heroes" labels.

## Media & Tie-Ins

### Comic books (DC)
- **Three prestige mini-series**, one per year of the line (1984, 1985, 1986), tying into the toys.
  - The **1984** series was **plotted by Jack Kirby**, who also drew covers and wrote/penciled the final issue; he went on to pencil the **1985** series.
  - Reprinted in *The Jack Kirby Omnibus Vol. 2* (2013), *Super Powers by Jack Kirby* (2018), and *DC Universe: Bronze Age Omnibus by Jack Kirby* (2019).
- **Pack-in mini-comics** — bundled with Series 1 and Series 2 figures (a distinct collecting sub-hobby; there are detailed checklists of these). Note that Shazam, Samurai, Golden Pharaoh, Cyborg, Cyclotron, and Plastic Man had **no English mini-comics** (some got Portuguese ones via Estrela).
- Continuity note: Super Powers was designed to be "continuity-free" and was later slotted into **Earth-32** by *Crisis on Infinite Earths: The Compendium*.

### Animated TV (the tie-in engine)
Hanna-Barbera's long-running *Super Friends* was **retitled to sync with the toy line**:
- **1984:** *SuperFriends: The Legendary Super Powers Show*
- **1985–86:** *The Super Powers Team: Galactic Guardians* (an 8-episode final season, story-edited by **Alan Burnett** — often cited as a precursor to the later DC animated "Timm-verse").

### Other tie-ins
- **Mayfair Games' *DC Heroes* RPG** launched in this era and tied into the branding.
- **Which Way Books** interactive storybooks.
- **VHS/Betamax:** Warner Home Video reissued 1960s Filmation DC cartoons (Superman, Batman, Superboy, Aquaman) under the Super Powers label in 1985 (also part of DC's 50th-anniversary push).
- **Massive merchandising blitz** (per a 1984 Toy Fair licensing handout): lunchboxes, Underoos, 3-D puffy stickers, party supplies, paintable figurines, bedsheets, clothing, and coloring/activity books (*Secret of the Frozen City*, plus character-specific titles).

### The creators to credit
- **Jack Kirby** (Fourth World designs + comics)
- **George Pérez** (Cyborg, Lex Luthor, Brainiac)
- **José Luis García-López** (the DC Style Guide art most packaging is based on)
- **Dick Giordano** (additional artwork)

## Competition & Legacy

- Mattel's **Secret Wars** (Marvel, late 1984) launched as a direct competitor — kicking off the enduring pattern of Marvel and DC using **rival toymakers**.
- In 1985, DC honored Kenner in its 50th-anniversary *Fifty Who Made DC Great*.
- **Toy Biz's *DC Comics Super Heroes*** (1989) borrowed heavily from Super Powers molds/designs (Superman, Robin, and Penguin were near-identical).
- Kenner reused Super Powers molds elsewhere (e.g., the *Robin Hood: Prince of Thieves* line).

### Later revivals of the brand
- **Kenner *Total Justice*** (1996) — Kenner's return to the wider DC roster.
- **Mattel** — a 30th-anniversary Super Powers revival (2014).
- **McFarlane Toys / DC Direct** — a modern Super Powers line beginning **2022** (Batman, Superman, Darkseid, the Batwing, Supermobile, Invisible Jet, and many more across ongoing waves). McFarlane even reissued the Argentine-style Riddler using a Green Lantern-type mold.
- **Tweeterhead** (Sideshow) — high-end Super Powers **maquette statues** (announced 2018).
- Nostalgia spin-offs: Monogram figural keychains (2016), Huckleberry "Yubi's Fingerines" (2016), a DecoPac Batcopter cake topper (2017), and more.

## Collecting Notes

- **Hardest to find:** Series 3 carded figures overall, with **Cyborg** and **Mister Miracle** the toughest US cards; the **Argentine Riddler** is the ultimate grail (~$2–3k).
- **Mail-away Clark Kent** is a prized Series 2 get.
- **Complete Hall of Justice** (boxed, with the landing-pad ramp) and the **Series 2 vehicles** (Boulder Bomber, Darkseid Destroyer) are condition-sensitive.
- **Condition watch:** the spring action mechanisms wear out — figures with leg-action (Flash, Aquaman) often become loose and hard to stand.
- **Completeness:** capes, weapons, armor, and the pack-in mini-comics dramatically affect value.
